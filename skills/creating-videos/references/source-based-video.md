# Source-Based Recaps, Adaptations, and Dubbing

Use this for recaps, highlight edits, translations, dubbed versions, and narrated adaptations built from existing video or audio. Keep every new statement and edit tied to the supplied source.

## Build a source map

1. Preserve original files and assign each source a stable ID. Record filename, duration, dimensions, frame rate, audio presence, and any known rights or usage limits.
2. Transcribe speech with timestamps when possible. Correct names, numbers, speaker attribution, and material recognition errors against the audio. Keep original language and translated text in separate fields.
3. Detect meaningful scene or shot changes and inspect key frames. Use the transcript and sampled frames together; speech alone cannot establish what is visible, and frames alone cannot establish what was said.
4. Create a source map with source ID, in/out timecode, visible action, spoken line or sound, confidence, and possible narrative role. Note ambiguity and do not fill gaps with invented action or dialogue.

## Decide the story and edit order

Before voiceover, define the intended audience, point of view, viewer promise, and what changes from opening to close. For each beat, record the source evidence, what the viewer should see, whether original dialogue/natural sound or narration leads, and why.

When the script must match selected source footage tightly, choose the usable clips and story order first, then write narration against that selected cut. Keep source in/out times visible through revisions. Preserve complete, context-accurate dialogue when it carries the story; prefer the original speaker or natural sound for a beat when it communicates more than added narration. Use silence intentionally. Do not narrate over a moment whose original sound should lead.

For an adaptation or translation that keeps the original story order, preserve sequence and meaning. For a recap that reorders events, ensure the new order remains understandable and does not change cause, chronology, speaker intent, or outcome. Mark any omitted context that would make a clip misleading.

## Translate and dub carefully

- Translate meaning, tone, names, technical terms, humor, and cultural references for the target audience; do not translate word-for-word when it becomes unnatural or distorts the claim.
- Keep each translated phrase linked to the source phrase and time range. Preserve qualifications, uncertainty, and who said what.
- Choose a target-language voice only after confirming pronunciation, voice rights, intended audience, and usage terms. Keep one coherent voice identity unless the source structure needs distinct speakers.
- Fit speech to the available timing without rushing to unintelligibility or stretching audio unnaturally. Prefer concise natural phrasing or a justified re-edit over aggressive speed changes.
- Keep original audio when requested or useful, duck it beneath the dub only when intelligible, and avoid phasey or distracting double speech. If replacing original speech, confirm that choice.
- Generate subtitles from the final dubbed audio or approved translated transcript, not stale source timings. Include a separate subtitle file when requested or useful.

## Review against source and deliver

Listen to the final dub with the source available for comparison. Check semantic accuracy, omissions/additions, speaker alignment, names, numbers, pronunciation, scene sync, captions, and sound balance. Review that selected footage matches the narration and that edits do not create false chronology or imply events not shown. Include a source-time map, transcript/translation, captions, edit timeline, and QA notes when useful. Confirm rights to edit, translate, dub, and distribute source media for the intended destination.
