# Analyze a Reference Video

Use this when the user supplies a video they like or asks for a new work inspired by a reference. The goal is to understand what makes it effective and transform those techniques for a new subject, audience, and brand.

## Inspect before proposing

Confirm the reference is accessible and authorized for analysis. Watch it once for its overall effect. Then inspect the transcript, scene boundaries, representative frames, edit points, and audio where tools allow. Record the observation method and note any inaccessible portions.

Describe evidence, not impressions alone:

- runtime, aspect ratio, and broad structure;
- opening hook and when the core promise becomes clear;
- sequence of narrative or information beats;
- shot lengths, pace changes, pauses, and transition patterns;
- composition, scale, camera movement, graphic movement, and visual hierarchy;
- caption and title behavior, density, readability, and safe-area use;
- sound layers, accents, music changes, silence, and sync points;
- repeated motifs and how the ending resolves or echoes the opening.

Do not infer unsupported intent or claim an exact technical measurement from visual estimation. Label estimated timings and subjective judgments.

## Translate technique into an original brief

Give the user two or three meaningfully different directions when the choice affects the result. For each, state:

- what technique it borrows (for example, pacing, contrast, shot progression, or sound rhythm);
- what changes (subject, script, visual metaphor, assets, colors, composition, and specific execution);
- why it fits the new audience and message;
- what source material and capabilities it needs;
- estimated duration, cost, and meaningful uncertainty when tool pricing applies;
- a small sample to review before scaling production.

Use the reference as analysis material, not as a source to copy. Do not reuse its footage, music, artwork, brand assets, exact script, or distinctive complete expression without permission. Do not imitate a living creator's signature style. Capture transferable principles and develop a distinct treatment. Keep reference screenshots or excerpts only when permitted, and record their origin and usage limits.

## Optional fidelity work

If the user explicitly asks to recreate or match a reference and has appropriate rights, define what “match” means: structure, motion timing, composition, colors, text, or frame-level similarity. Separate source-media matching from rendering and output checks. Compare the result at corresponding timecodes, including transition boundaries and first/last frames of shots. Report what was measured and what was judged by eye; never imply pixel-level fidelity from sparse frame samples.
