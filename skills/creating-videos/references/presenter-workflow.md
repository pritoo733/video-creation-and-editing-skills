# Presenter and Talking-Head Workflow

Use this optional workflow for real or synthetic presenters, interviews, narration-led talking heads, and videos with lip synchronization. It can be combined with screen recordings, B-roll, diagrams, or animation. It is not a default for every video format.

## Choose the presentation route

Match the approach to the brief and available material:

- **Presenter-led:** a person or authorized digital presenter carries the explanation; supporting visuals clarify selected points.
- **Screen demonstration:** the product or process is the evidence; keep important UI large and readable, with a presenter used only where it adds context.
- **Mixed explainer:** narration leads while footage, diagrams, or motion graphics carry each idea that they can show more clearly.
- **Voiceover without a presenter:** useful when a face adds little, no suitable authorized portrait exists, or the topic is better demonstrated visually.

Explain meaningful tradeoffs such as format, portrait requirements, paid generation, accessibility, and viewer expectations. Do not silently switch to a synthetic presenter because it is convenient. Ask for a choice if route selection materially changes the result and cannot be inferred from the brief.

## Confirm rights and privacy before generation

- Confirm rights to use a real or synthetic presenter's image, name, performance, and likeness for this project and destination.
- Confirm explicit authorization before cloning a voice or uploading a face, voice sample, or confidential footage to an external service. Never infer voice identity from an image.
- Tell the user which source files will be sent, to which capability/provider, and what cost or usage is known before the first paid or remote generation. If price is unknown, say so and keep the pilot small.
- Keep credentials and access tokens out of prompts, project records, logs, screenshots, and deliveries. Record sanitized settings only.

## Lock content and audio before final visuals

1. Write for the chosen presentation route. Keep one clear idea per beat; allow breaths and visual pauses. For a short topic explainer, a practical shape is hook, promise, a few useful beats, synthesis, and close, adapted to the requested duration.
2. Approve and save the spoken script separately from stage directions, captions, and on-screen text. Mark claims that remain unverified.
3. Create the complete narration with one approved voice identity and configuration. Audition a short representative passage for pronunciation, cadence, tone, and intelligibility before making the full track.
4. Compare the final recording against the approved script by listening or using speech recognition. Correct omitted or repeated words, names, numbers, pronunciation, and unexpected speech. Save the approved audio and its actual duration; this audio becomes the timing reference.
5. After any narration change, update word or phrase timings, presenter clips, scene durations, and captions that depend on the old audio.

## Pilot, generate, and preserve continuity

- Make the smallest useful pilot before a full paid run. Test representative speech and framing; for lip-synced output, inspect identity, mouth timing, face, hands, lighting, and sound. Approve its quality before scaling.
- Set a retry ceiling in advance. If a pilot fails, identify the cause and change one relevant variable at a time. Stop and report when the ceiling is reached or when a material change in cost, privacy, voice, appearance, or provider is needed.
- When a service limits clip duration, split at natural pauses using the final audio's sentence or word timings. Avoid cuts through speech. Record every segment's source range and destination start time.
- Keep identity, voice, framing, camera, lighting, wardrobe, background, and motion instructions consistent across segments. Inspect seams for jumps in pose, expression, light, mouth position, or background; cover only with an intentional cut, layout change, or supporting visual.
- Use one continuous source when it produces better continuity and meets tool limits. Keep presenter video muted in the edit when approved narration is the program audio, preventing duplicate or drifting dialogue.

## Keep a resumable production record

Record evidence as the project advances; do not mark a stage complete merely because a tool was called. A compact job log or project manifest should link to:

1. brief, route, permissions, and approved script;
2. narration file, voice configuration, duration, and transcript comparison;
3. visual plan and reviewed pilot;
4. generation/edit source assets, segment timings, and accepted takes;
5. timeline, captions, render, and technical/visual review notes;
6. final exports and delivery record.

For generated work, record provider/tool, model or version when known, non-secret settings, task/job ID, date, cost evidence or estimate, and whether the result was accepted. On resume, inspect existing outputs and job status first. Reuse accepted work and check whether interrupted jobs completed before resubmitting, so retries do not create duplicate charges or inconsistent takes.

## Compose and review for a human viewer

- Let audio timing drive semantic cuts, captions, and lip-sync. Keep each clip's timeline start, displayed duration, and source in-point as distinct values in an editable timeline or manifest.
- Establish the subject and viewer payoff early. Design the opening frame deliberately; keep titles and graphics clear of the face, eyes, mouth, hands, captions, and platform controls.
- Add one useful visual idea per beat. Use callouts only on a spoken emphasis or useful evidence point; vary them with purpose and avoid covering the presenter or screen evidence.
- End with a complete thought and only the requested next step. Leave a natural pause after the last word; do not append an unrequested promotional CTA.
- Review the full export at normal speed when possible. For presenter footage check identity consistency, lip-sync, blink and gesture naturalness, hands, lighting, crop, frozen sections, seams, audio sync, captions, and final-word/tail behavior. Clearly report when the review used samples rather than full playback.
