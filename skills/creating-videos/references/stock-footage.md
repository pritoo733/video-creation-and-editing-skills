# Source footage for its role in the story

Write shot-specific searches: subject + action + setting + framing. For example, “hands reviewing a building plan, close-up” supports project planning better than a generic skyline. Source owned footage first when the actual product, people, or results matter. Stock illustrates context; do not present stock models as employees, customers, or endorsers, or stock editing software as a feature of the promoted product.

Inspect the chosen interval for relevance, focus, movement, crop flexibility, continuity, brands, people, and visual artifacts. Native HD/4K dimensions alone do not establish quality. Prefer connected wide/action/detail shots over unrelated attractive clips. Confirm enough handles for the intended transition and keep useful motion rather than looping a short clip to fill narration.

## Sources and license checks

Verified against these official pages on 2026-10-15; recheck terms for each new production:

| Source | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| [Pexels license](https://www.pexels.com/legal-pages/license/) | Personal/commercial use and modification are generally allowed. Respect endorsement, recognizable content, unaltered resale, and redistribution restrictions. API terms can add separate requirements. |
| [Pixabay content license](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/) | Check prohibited uses, trademarks, people, standalone distribution, and other rights that may apply to the chosen content. |
| [Mixkit licenses](https://mixkit.co/license/) | Inspect the individual asset page: **Video Free** permits commercial projects; **Video Restricted** is personal/noncommercial. A free download button or a free category listing does not settle this. |

Record the original asset page, creator/asset ID, downloaded variant, license URL, verification date, saved evidence, planned use, attribution obligations, and additional restrictions. Save checks with the project; a machine-readable `reviewed` flag is a record of the operator's decision, not independent legal verification. Check music, fonts, voice models, and sound effects separately.

Use supported APIs or the provider's offered download route for selected assets. Do not scrape whole libraries, bypass access controls, strip watermarks, or treat a sponsored paid listing as free stock. If an appropriate clip is unavailable, choose another licensed shot or a deliberate original graphic; do not silently replace a footage-led brief with slides.

Distribute the edited video and useful attribution/source notes. Do not commit or bundle a reusable collection of raw stock media unless the applicable terms explicitly permit that distribution. Keep credentials and private footage outside the repository.
