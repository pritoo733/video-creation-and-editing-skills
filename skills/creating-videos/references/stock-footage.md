# Source footage for its role in the story

For promos, ads, and other footage-friendly stories, plan a mixed or footage-led sequence instead of defaulting to graphics. Use stock when it shows a specific real-world action or environment that advances the narration; use owned screen captures for actual product behavior and graphics for abstract relationships. Do not substitute stock for proof of product performance, customer outcomes, or named people.

Write shot-specific searches with at least three meaningful subject/action/context terms: subject + action + setting, with framing only as a secondary detail. For example, “loan officer reviewing property appraisal file” supports a lending-process line better than “cinematic business skyline.” Avoid mood-only terms such as “professional,” “beautiful,” or “high quality”: they retrieve attractive but story-irrelevant clips. Keep the `query`, visible `action`, and `why_it_fits` aligned to one exact narration beat. Source owned footage first when the actual product, people, or results matter. Stock illustrates context; do not present stock models as employees, customers, or endorsers, or stock editing software as a feature of the promoted product.

The automatic runner searches a wider Commons candidate set and ranks it by overlap between specific query terms and asset titles/descriptions. It rejects candidates with fewer than two matching terms or a weak score. This metadata score is only a relevance filter, not proof that the clip depicts the right action. Inspect several frames across the selected interval and reject it if the action, setting, people, or composition does not support the spoken line. Try a more concrete query before choosing an original graphic; never lower the match bar to fill the timeline.

Inspect the chosen interval for relevance, focus, movement, crop flexibility, continuity, brands, people, and visual artifacts. Confirm that the visible action actually supports the narration; a matching keyword, title, or preview still is insufficient. Native HD/4K dimensions alone do not establish quality. Prefer connected wide/action/detail shots over unrelated attractive clips. Confirm enough handles for the intended transition and keep useful motion rather than looping a short clip to fill narration.

## Sources and license checks

Verified against these official pages on 2026-10-15; recheck terms for each new production:

| Source | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| [Pexels license](https://www.pexels.com/legal-pages/license/) | Personal/commercial use and modification are generally allowed. Respect endorsement, recognizable content, unaltered resale, and redistribution restrictions. API terms can add separate requirements. |
| [Pixabay content license](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/) | Check prohibited uses, trademarks, people, standalone distribution, and other rights that may apply to the chosen content. |
| [Mixkit licenses](https://mixkit.co/license/) | Inspect the individual asset page: **Video Free** permits commercial projects; **Video Restricted** is personal/noncommercial. A free download button or a free category listing does not settle this. |

Record the original asset page, creator/asset ID, downloaded variant, license URL, verification date, saved evidence, planned use, attribution obligations, and additional restrictions. Save checks with the project; a machine-readable `reviewed` flag is a record of the operator's decision, not independent legal verification. Check music, fonts, voice models, and sound effects separately.

Use supported APIs or the provider's offered download route for selected assets. Do not scrape whole libraries, bypass access controls, strip watermarks, or treat a sponsored paid listing as free stock. If an appropriate clip is unavailable, choose another licensed shot or a deliberate original graphic; do not silently replace a footage-led brief with slides.

Distribute the edited video and useful attribution/source notes. Do not commit or bundle a reusable collection of raw stock media unless the applicable terms explicitly permit that distribution. Keep credentials and private footage outside the repository.
