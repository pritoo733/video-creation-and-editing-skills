# Research and Scripting

Use this reference for factual videos, current topics, product explainers, marketing videos, documentaries, and education. For a personal or purely expressive edit, research only what the user wants checked.

## Research workflow

1. Convert the brief into answerable questions. Separate background context from claims that will appear in narration or on screen.
2. Use supplied material first when it is the intended source. For new or unstable facts, browse current sources. Prefer original documentation, official data, first-party statements, research papers, and direct records appropriate to the claim.
3. Maintain a source ledger: source title, URL or file, author/publisher, publication/update date, access date, and the exact script claim it supports.
4. Cross-check material claims, statistics, dates, product steps, quotations, names, and terms. Treat search snippets and summaries as leads; inspect the underlying source.
5. Mark each note as verified fact, disputed, inference, or creative framing. Keep disputed claims out of the final script or state the uncertainty clearly.
6. Keep citations in research notes and add concise on-screen attribution or end credits when the context requires viewers to see the source.

Research depth should match the video's claims and audience. Do not add research merely to make a simple personal montage sound authoritative. Do not expose confidential source documents to external research or generation services without authorization.

## Script workflow

Write a one-line premise, a viewer promise, and the intended ending before drafting. Choose a structure that fits the mode: opening hook, setup, ordered explanation or story beats, transitions, payoff, and next step. Personal stories can use chronological, thematic, or memory-based structure; preserve what the owner actually shared.

For every scene, record:

- scene purpose and target duration
- spoken copy or dialogue, separate from production directions
- visual/sound plan and available source asset
- supporting research/source IDs for factual claims
- on-screen text, captions, and attribution
- transition into and out of the scene

Write for the ear. Read the lines aloud or audition TTS; replace long clauses, awkward lists, repeated setup, and unclear pronouns. Match runtime to the brief, leaving pauses for important visuals. Do not pad a script to increase runtime.

Before production, verify the full script against the source ledger and brief. Confirm every claim has support, the voice fits the audience, the ending resolves the opening, and each planned scene has a feasible visual. Flag claims awaiting approval and prepare a version that omits them so production can continue safely.
