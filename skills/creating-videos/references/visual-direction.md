# Visual Direction and Originality

Use this guide before producing a video when look, pacing, or motion materially affects the result. It applies to live footage, screen recordings, animation, motion graphics, AI-generated media, and mixed formats.

## Make a visual treatment

Write a concise treatment that answers:

- What is the visual idea that connects the video from opening to ending?
- What should viewers feel, notice, or understand from the imagery?
- What palette, lighting, contrast, texture, typography, and framing fit the subject and audience?
- How will shots, graphics, and camera or graphic motion change over time?
- What is the transition rule: hard cuts, match cuts, dissolves, wipes, or a deliberate mix?
- What is the sound palette, including room tone, natural sound, voice, music, and effects?
- Which visual conventions, references, or brand rules should guide the work, and what should be original?
- Which production limits (source footage, aspect ratio, accessibility, render tools, time, budget) shape the design?

Tie choices to the brief. A calm personal story may need held images and natural sound; a software tutorial may need stable captures, highlighted controls, and enough reading time. Do not add motion, music, texture, or effects just to make a static sequence feel busy.

For motion-led work, add a one-sentence **motion thesis**: the visual metaphor or changing state that carries the central idea through time. Then map the script into a **beat graph**: for each beat, state what viewers see at the beginning, what changes, what they should notice, and what state leads into the next beat. Reuse [the visual beat sheet](../../../templates/visual-beat-sheet.md) when it helps.

## Check the look before scaling

Create one representative frame, opening, or short scene before producing many assets. Include the intended title or caption treatment, a real source visual, typical motion, and representative sound when possible. Inspect it at the expected delivery size and ask:

- Is the main subject or action clear immediately?
- Does the composition direct attention to the important detail?
- Are titles and captions readable on a phone and within platform safe areas?
- Do motion and transitions communicate structure, or do they distract?
- Does this look specific to this subject and audience rather than like an unchosen default template?
- Are visual references used as inspiration without copying protected footage, artwork, or a living creator's distinctive style?

Revise the treatment and sample before applying it to the full video when any answer reveals a material problem. For long videos, establish a small set of scene patterns and vary them intentionally while keeping typography, color, and motion coherent.

For videos that promise animation or cinematic motion, check for static-slide drift: each scene should have a meaningful visual event, state change, camera move, or purposeful hold. If motion does not clarify an idea or feeling, remove it. Purposeful stillness is valid when the treatment calls for it.

## Keep variation purposeful

Plan visual changes around meaning: new evidence, a new speaker, a change in time or place, a step in a process, or a shift in emotional beat. Maintain continuity of people, objects, screen state, geography, lighting, and time when the story implies continuity. For generated scenes, track reference images and prompt intent, then inspect identity, anatomy, text, object persistence, flicker, and temporal consistency.

For data and instructional graphics, preserve exact values and labels. Do not use decorative motion that changes a chart's apparent meaning, implies false precision, or makes comparisons misleading. Use captions and diagrams to clarify the narration, not duplicate every spoken word by default.

## Record the treatment

Keep the chosen treatment, references, palette, fonts, motion rules, transition rules, sound plan, and approval notes in the production brief or project folder. Record any deviations that materially affect continuity. Make sure all reference assets and fonts are cleared for the intended use.
